— the developing tray —
what this room rests on

this is a sand tray, of the kind a therapist keeps on a low table: a bound tray of damp sand and a box of figures beside it. you place the people, animals and things of your world in the sand and arrange them until the arrangement says what you could not.

dora kalff — who built her trays for children who had no words yet — called it “the free and protected space”: nothing you set in the sand is judged, nothing is interpreted to your face, and no figure is more approved than another. the three photographs on the wall are yours to fill in order, one scene, then a second, then a third — a story in three frames.

the machines along the back wall are this room's own magic: things shrink or grow, and one of them prints what a figure looks like when only its shadow is believed. use them as the story asks.

when all three frames hang, the room shows you the story and offers it to the journal as one artifact.
